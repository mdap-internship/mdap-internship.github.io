Why I joined MDAP

As a science major, I’ve spent a lot of time learning how to produce data, running experiments, collecting results, recording observations. But somewhere along the way I realised I wasn’t really sure what to do with data once I had it. How do you analyse it properly? How do you make it mean something beyond the spreadsheet it lives in? That gap bothered me. So when the opportunity came up to intern with the Melbourne Data Analytics Platform, it felt like exactly the right place to start filling that gap. A research environment, real datasets, and the chance to learn something I genuinely didn’t know how to do yet. That’s why I chose MDAP.

Project Overview

Suburban Futures is a research project based at the University of Melbourne, led by Amanda Belton and Emily Fitzgerald in collaboration with the Faculty of Architecture, Building and Planning, Melbourne School of Design.

The project is essentially about Melbourne’s suburbs, understanding them better, celebrating them, and finding the stories that sit inside the data. It draws on publicly available sources like the Australian Bureau of Statistics and Data Vic to explore the patterns and trends that shape suburban life across Greater Melbourne.

My Role

When I first started, I honestly wasn’t sure where to begin. I had datasets in front of me, a general direction, and not much else. A big part of the early weeks was just figuring out what was actually worth exploring, which datasets had enough depth, which questions were interesting enough to pursue, and which ones were dead ends. Once I had something to work with, the challenge was to take that raw data and turning it into something visual. I had never built an interactive map before. I didn’t know how to clean a dataset properly, or how to make two different data sources talk to each other. There was a lot of trial and error, a lot of asking my supervisors for help, and a lot of moments where I wasn’t sure it was going to work. But it did. And by the end, I had gone from not knowing where to start, to being able to independently take a dataset, work out what it was saying, and build something that made that visible.

Achievements & Learnings

Across my internship, I built interactive maps covering public transport usage, cultural diversity, migration patterns, and rental affordability across Greater Melbourne. The rental map was my most significant output. It visualises median rent across 31 local government areas, every quarter, from 2000 to 2025. What the data showed was that rental pressure in Melbourne is not confined to the inner city, it has spread broadly across the suburbs, including areas that were considered affordable not long ago. That finding became much more tangible once it was visible on a map rather than sitting in a spreadsheet.

I had to face a few technical challenges which included learning how to do the visualisations, how to clean, standardise, and join data before it could be visualised. I overcame these issues with the help of my supervisors, consulting documentation, and testing outputs at each step.

My supervisors guided me through all of it, and I came away with a much clearer understanding of what it actually takes to go from a raw dataset to something meaningful and accessible.

Research Outputs

Time-Slider Rental Map (2000–2025) An interactive choropleth map visualising median rent across 31 Greater Melbourne local government areas, spanning 25 years of quarterly data from Victoria’s Residential Tenancies Bond Authority. The map allows users to move through time and observe how rental pressure, once concentrated in Melbourne’s inner eastern suburbs, has gradually spread outward into growth corridors that were previously considered affordable. Built using quantile classification to ensure geographic differences remain visible across a wide rent range, with hover tooltips showing each LGA’s median rent, its deviation from the Melbourne median, and year-on-year change.

Suburb Ethnicity & Migration Map (2021) An interactive, multi-layer map visualising the geographic distribution of cultural communities across Greater Melbourne, built using 2021 ABS Census data joined to suburb-level boundary files. Ten ancestry layers, including Chinese, Indian, Vietnamese, Greek, Italian and Lebanese communities which can be toggled independently, revealing that Melbourne’s diversity is not evenly spread but spatially precise- each community occupies a distinct part of the city, shaped by arrival history, housing affordability, and existing community networks.
